\section{Q2}
Prove the following statement. 
\begin{equation*}
    \forall x \in \N, \exists y \in \N, \paren{x^2 + 2 - y} \mid \paren{10 - x^2 - y^2}
\end{equation*}
\begin{solution}
    Let $x$ be arbitrary number in $\N$. We select $y$ to be $x^2 + 1$ in $\N$. 

    subtituting $y = x^2 + 1$ into the expression, we 
    \begin{equation*}
        \paren{x^2 + 2 - x^2 - 1} \mid \paren{10 - x^2 - \paren{x^2 + 1}^2}
    \end{equation*}

    This implies 
    \begin{equation*}
        \paren{1} \mid \paren{10 - x^2 - x^4 - 2x^2 - 1 }, 
    \end{equation*}

    which equals to 
    \begin{equation*}
        \paren{1} \mid \paren{-x^4 - 3x^2 + 9}
    \end{equation*}

    there exists a number $k = -x^4 - 3x^2 + 9$ in $\Z$ such that 
    \begin{equation*}
        (-x^4 - 3x^2 + 9) \cdot 1 =  -x^4 - 3x^2 + 9
    \end{equation*}

    for all $x \in \N$. 

    Thus, according to the definition of divisibility, 
    \begin{equation*}
        \forall x \in \N, \exists y \in \N, \paren{x^2 + 2 - y} \mid \paren{10 - x^2 - y^2}. 
    \end{equation*}
\end{solution}
